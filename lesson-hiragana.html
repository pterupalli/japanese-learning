Foundation
What is Hiragana?
Hiragana is one of the three writing systems in Japanese. It is a syllabic alphabet — every character represents a complete syllable sound, not just a consonant or vowel.

It is used for native Japanese words, grammatical particles (は, が, を), verb endings, and furigana (pronunciation guides above kanji).

There are 46 base characters. Once you know them all, you can phonetically read and write any Japanese word.
あ
Pure syllables
Each character = one sound. あ = "ah", い = "ee", う = "oo". No ambiguity — one symbol, one sound, always.
書
Easy stroke order
Most hiragana have 2–4 strokes. Stroke order matters — it affects how characters connect in handwriting and how they look natural.
読
Read anything
Children's books, restaurant menus, grammar textbooks — all written in hiragana first. Master this and Japan opens up.
Method
How to use this lesson
01
Click any character below to open the study popup
The popup shows pronunciation, mouth position, stroke order, a memory mnemonic, and example words. Audio plays automatically.
02
Study it, then mark it "Learning" or "Mastered"
Mark as Learning while you're still reviewing it. Mark as Mastered once you can recall it instantly without looking. +5 XP for each mastered.
03
Do the vowel row first: あ い う え お
These 5 pure vowel sounds underpin every other character. If you know them cold, every other row becomes easier because the vowel sound stays the same.
04
One row per day — don't rush all 46 at once
10 rows, 5 characters each. One row per day = 10 days to complete hiragana. That pace allows proper consolidation in long-term memory.
