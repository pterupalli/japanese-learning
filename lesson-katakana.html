What is it
What is Katakana?
Katakana is the second of Japan's phonetic alphabets. It has the exact same 46 sounds as hiragana — only the shapes differ. Think of hiragana as cursive and katakana as print.

Katakana is used for foreign loanwords (コーヒー = coffee), foreign names, scientific terms, onomatopoeia, and for emphasis (like italics in English).

Once you know hiragana, katakana is much faster to learn because your brain already knows all the sounds — you just need new shapes.
ア
Same sounds, new shapes
ア = "ah" (same as あ). Every sound is identical to hiragana — only the visual form changes. Angular vs. rounded.
コ
Read foreign words
コーヒー (koohii) = coffee. マクドナルド = McDonald's. Katakana unlocks an entire layer of Japanese vocabulary instantly.
ン
Menus and signage
Most Japanese menus, product labels, and brand names use katakana. Mastering it means you can read real-world Japanese immediately.
